01 / The research question

Motion is measurable.
Is it understood?

Pose sequences contain useful information about walking. GaitEval asks whether language models can use that evidence to predict dataset-specific labels, rather than relying on task priors.

Coordinates, motion summaries and biomechanical measurements offer different views of the same motion. Feature-matched linear references, controlled inputs and task-specific adaptation help explain the predictions.

04 / What we find

Evidence matters.
So does the task.

01

Zero-shot agreement is limited.

Most tested motion-summary tasks show limited zero-shot agreement, even though numerical references recover useful signal from the same features.

02

Adaptation recovers signal.

Few-shot prompting and LoRA adaptation improve in-distribution predictions in selected settings. Gains depend on the dataset and input representation.

03

Transfer remains unreliable.

The tested bidirectional ATAXIA–PATHOGAIT binary probe does not show reliable transfer. This finding is specific to that harmonized dataset pair.
